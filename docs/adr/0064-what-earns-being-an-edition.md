# An edition exists when it changes what you consume or how

A different translation, cut, narration or medium is an edition. A different cover, printing or
ISBN is a category or identifier statement ON an edition, never an edition of its own.

This rule is load-bearing because of the one-level collapse. Under BIBFRAME every printing is
legitimately an Instance, so ONE Harry Potter novel has several hundred: 80-plus translations, two
texts, six cover treatments, illustrated, anniversary, digital, three audio. A books provider will
return all of them.

## The contrast that makes the line visible

A RADIO DRAMATISATION WITH A REWRITTEN SCRIPT IS AN ADAPTATION, and a separate item. Production
scale, a large cast and original music do NOT cross the line. A rewritten script does. Ask what
changed: the content, or the rendering.

A PUBLISHED SCREENPLAY IS AN EDITION of its film, medium text — the same content in another form.
*Fantastic Beasts: The Original Screenplay* was edited by its own publisher to match the final
theatrical cut, which settles a case that could otherwise be argued either way.

Both from the Harry Potter pass, 2026-09-05.

Excess editions are DROPPED AT THE DOOR AND COUNTED, exactly as unmatched fields are, so the owner
is told the number and decides. Without it the catalogue's most famous item has several hundred
editions, one arbitrary default, and an edition picker nobody can use.
