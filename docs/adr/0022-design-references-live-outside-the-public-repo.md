# Replicas and the design prototype live outside the public repo

Every replica (`GLOSSARY.md`: untitled.stream's web replica, its app replica for the iPhone and Mac apps, the Mac app added on 3 Oct 2026 by CC-36, and Brink's; the MD Vinyl, America.gov and tvOS replicas were dropped on 3 Oct 2026, their specs CC-35, CC-40 and CC-34 canceled) and the design prototype live in their own repositories under `~/canoncore/`, each with a private GitHub remote as a backup, and never in CanonCore's repository. One replica is the exception to `~/canoncore/`: AppleMusic27, the Owner's replica of Apple Music, lives at `~/orca/projects/AppleMusic27` in its own private organisation (`jacobrees-applemusic`) and Linear workspace, and is a design reference by ADR 0025 (the Owner, 2 Nov 2026). This is because CanonCore's repository is public (`gh repo view`, 2026-09-30), so anything pushed to it, a branch included, is published. These works reproduce other people's products, whose terms may forbid derivative works (untitled's do; its makers permitted extraction, amended 6 Oct 2026). The private repositories hold, or will hold once the app replica and Brink's are built, the files their rebuilds load, taken from the apps and sites, among them 2016 Untitled Sans files under Klim's Test Font Licence (the web replica's five committed woff2 files today, the app's three OTFs later), a further reason they stay private. They may hold code not yet licensed for the AGPL (ADR 0019), and the prototype's data snapshot carries Providers' artwork fetched with the Owner's personal keys. Only what the prototype decides reaches CanonCore's main branch: DESIGN.md, the verdicts on each spec, and the picked variants described in words and tokens. Where a replica keeps its raw material outside git is said by `GLOSSARY.md`'s Replica, not by this record. (Amended 15 Nov 2026, the Owner: it said "recreation" for what `GLOSSARY.md` now calls a replica, AppleMusic27 included.)

## Considered Options

- The prototype on a `prototype/design` branch of CanonCore (D-66 of the first grill): rejected, because on a public repository a pushed branch is a publication.
- Local repositories with no remote, as untitled's web replica began: rejected, because a dead disk would lose weeks of work.
- One shared repository for every replica: rejected in favour of one each, so each keeps its own tooling and none waits on another's.
